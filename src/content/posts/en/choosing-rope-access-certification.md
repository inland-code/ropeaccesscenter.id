This question reaches our desk almost every week, from two directions at once: workers who want to start a career at height, and HSE managers who have to meet contract requirements. Both usually arrive at the same question: TKPK or TKBT?

## Two schemes, one regulation

Both are work-at-height certifications issued by Indonesia’s Ministry of Manpower (Kemnaker RI) under Regulation 9/2016. The difference lies in **how** the worker reaches and stays at the work position.

**TKPK (Tenaga Kerja pada Ketinggian)** is the rope access scheme: the worker hangs on two rope systems — a working line and a safety line — and moves up, down, or across along the rope. TKPK has three levels, from [Level 1 technician](/en/tkpk-level-1-training/) to [Level 3 supervisor](/en/tkpk-level-3-training/).

**TKBT (Tenaga Kerja Bangunan Tinggi)** is the scheme for climbing structures — towers, steel frames, scaffolds, poles — using fall arrest such as twin lanyards. TKBT has two levels: [Level 1](/en/tkbt-level-1-training/) and [Level 2](/en/tkbt-level-2-training/).

## A quick way to choose

Ask one question: **does the worker hang on a rope, or stand and climb on the structure?**

- Cleaning glass and repairing [building façades](/en/building-facade-maintenance/), painting tank walls, inspecting chimney sides — the worker hangs on rope. That is **TKPK** territory.
- Climbing telecom towers, working on steel frames, erecting scaffolds — the worker climbs the structure and moves between anchors. That is **TKBT** territory.

Many real jobs mix the two. A technician who climbs a tower and then descends on rope to reach an antenna, for example, should hold both certificates.

## For technicians: where to start

If you are starting out, pick the scheme that matches the work you are most likely to get. High-rise building maintenance and industry need a lot of TKPK; telecoms and steel construction need a lot of TKBT.

Once you are working and building up documented hours, move up a level. Log every job from day one — experience that is not recorded is hard to prove when you apply for the next grade. We cover the ladder in our [rope access career map](/en/rope-access-career-indonesia/).

## For HSE managers: read the contract, not the brochure

The most expensive mistake we see is certifying a whole team on a scheme that does not fit the work. Before booking [training](/en/training/), check three things: the method of work on site, the competence requirements in the contract or permit to work, and the team structure — who works and who supervises.

For rope access teams, make sure a competent supervisor is on every site. For high-risk work, plan [rescue capability on site](/en/standby-rescue-services/) as well.

## A certificate is not the end

Any [certificate](/en/certification/) only states that someone met a standard on the day of the test. What keeps people safe is daily habit: pre-use equipment checks, a rescue plan that has actually been tested, and a culture in which anyone may stop the work.
