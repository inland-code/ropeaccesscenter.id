Rope access is a method of working at height in which workers reach, stay at, and move around the work position by hanging on rope. It is used to clean building glass, repair façades, paint tanks, and inspect chimneys and towers.

## The two-rope principle

The defining feature of rope access is **two independent rope systems**: a working line that supports the worker, and a safety line fitted with a fall arrest device. Each is anchored to its own anchor point. If one system fails, the other still holds the worker — which is what makes the method safe when it is done properly.

Workers wear a harness and use a descender, ascenders, and a fall arrester. All equipment is checked before use and logged periodically, as we explain in [inspecting rope access PPE](/en/rope-access-ppe-inspection/).

## When rope access is the right choice

Rope access is usually most efficient when:

- work points are spread out and hard to reach with scaffolding or a gondola, such as façade recesses, canopies, or tank sides;
- access must be set up quickly — a rope system is rigged in hours, not days;
- the area below cannot be disrupted for long, for example in a building that stays open.

Scaffolding is still better for heavy work that stays at one point for a long time. We compare the costs in [rope access versus scaffolding](/en/rope-access-vs-scaffolding-inspection-cost/), and compare it with a gondola on our [façade maintenance](/en/building-facade-maintenance/) page.

## The rules in Indonesia

Work at height is governed by Ministry of Manpower Regulation 9/2016, which requires planning, work procedures, suitable equipment, and competent workers. Technical guidance on rope access work was also issued in Director-General Decree 45/2008.

Rope access workers prove their competence with a Kemnaker certificate and K3 licence under the **TKPK** scheme, which has three grades — from [TKPK Level 1](/en/tkpk-level-1-training/) for beginners to [TKPK Level 3](/en/tkpk-level-3-training/) for supervisors. For climbing structures such as towers and steel frames, the scheme is **TKBT**. See our [certification](/en/certification/) page for details.

For building examples — glass cleaning, sealant, and façade care — see our group article [what is rope access](https://inland.co.id/en/what-is-rope-access/) on the Inland site.

## Safety is more than equipment

Good equipment is only half the answer. Every rope access job needs a risk assessment, a competent supervisor on site, and a [rescue plan that has been tested](/en/tested-rescue-plan/). Without them, even the best two-rope system cannot guarantee that everyone goes home safely.
