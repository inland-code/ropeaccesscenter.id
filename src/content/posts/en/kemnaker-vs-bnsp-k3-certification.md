"Work-at-height certificate" can mean two different things in Indonesia: a K3 certificate and licence from the **Ministry of Manpower (Kemnaker)**, or a competency certificate from the **National Professional Certification Agency (BNSP)**. Both are legitimate, but they come from different systems — and employers sometimes accept only one.

## The Kemnaker K3 certificate and licence

Kemnaker issues K3 certificates and licences for specific safety roles, including workers at height under Regulation 9/2016. The scheme is graded: **TKPK** Levels 1–3 for rope access and **TKBT** Levels 1–2 for climbing structures. Candidates train, then sit a competency test under Kemnaker rules.

These documents are often checked by labour inspectors and are a permit-to-work requirement on many construction, building, and industrial projects.

## The BNSP competency certificate

BNSP is an independent body that carries out work competency certification through licensed professional certification bodies (LSP), based on the national competency standards (SKKNI) under the national competency standardisation system. BNSP certificates are issued after assessment by an LSP assessor.

## Which one do you need?

There is no single answer — what matters is **who is asking**:

- For permits and on-site safety checks, many project owners ask for the Kemnaker K3 certificate and licence at the right grade.
- Some tenders or companies also list a BNSP competency certificate as an administrative requirement.

So before booking training, read the competence clause in the contract or tender documents first. For choosing between the rope access and high-structure schemes, see [TKPK or TKBT?](/en/choosing-rope-access-certification/).
