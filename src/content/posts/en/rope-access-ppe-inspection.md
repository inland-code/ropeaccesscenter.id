Most technicians check their kit every morning. Far fewer can produce a harness’s full history when an [auditor](/en/work-at-height-audit-checklist/) asks for it. Both matter equally.

## The pre-use check

Done daily, by the person who will wear it. Run your fingers along the full length of the webbing — fingers find broken fibres and hard spots that the eye misses. Check the stitching, especially at load-bearing points. Open and close every carabiner to confirm the gate returns fully and the lock engages.

For descenders and ascenders, check rope groove wear, cam cleanliness, and spring action. A device that catches slightly today will catch more next month.

## The six-monthly detailed inspection

This is done by a [competent](/en/tkpk-level-3-training/), recorded person — not by the user. Every item is checked against the manufacturer’s criteria, the result is logged, and the item is marked with its status. A simple colour-tape system per period makes status readable at a glance on site.

## When an item must retire

Four non-negotiable reasons: it has arrested a fall, it has suffered chemical or heat damage, it has passed the manufacturer’s age limit, or its history is unknown. That last one is often waved away — borrowed kit with no history must be treated as unserviceable.

## A record system that survives

Paper logbooks get lost. Spreadsheets branch into five versions. What survives is a system with three properties: every item carries a unique serial physically marked on it, every entry names who inspected it, and the records live in the same place as the rest of your HSE documentation.

When the auditor asks, you should not be searching — you should be opening.
