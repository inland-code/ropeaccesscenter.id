TKBT — Tenaga Kerja Bangunan Tinggi — is the Kemnaker certification scheme for workers who climb and work on structures: telecom towers, steel frames, scaffolds, and poles. It has two grades. If your work hangs on a rope, you need TKPK instead; we explain the difference in [TKPK or TKBT?](/en/choosing-rope-access-certification/).

## TKBT Level 1: working safely under supervision

[TKBT Level 1](/en/tkbt-level-1-training/) is the starting point. It focuses on personal safety: wearing and checking a full body harness, twin lanyards, and fall arresters; climbing and changing anchors while always attached (100% tie-off); and basic rescue and first aid.

Level 1 holders work under the supervision of a Level 2 holder. No previous experience is needed — the main requirements are being at least 18 and medically fit for work at height.

## TKBT Level 2: working independently and supervising

[TKBT Level 2](/en/tkbt-level-2-training/) adds responsibility for other people and for the work plan:

- assessing structural, weather, and electrical hazards before climbing;
- selecting and installing anchors and temporary lifelines;
- planning safe work steps and leading a rescue from a structure;
- supervising Level 1 workers.

It requires a TKBT Level 1 certificate and documented experience of work at height.

## When to step up

Move up to Level 2 when you work on structures routinely, are comfortable with the basics without reminders, and are starting to lead a small team. Keep a record of every job from the start — date, location, type of work, and supervisor’s name — because that record is your evidence of experience when you enrol.

## For companies

Build teams with a healthy ratio: every group of Level 1 workers on a structure needs a Level 2 supervisor present on site. For a group from one company, training can run at your site — ask through our [training](/en/training/) page.
