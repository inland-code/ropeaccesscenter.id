Rope access training is an investment — for technicians and for companies sending their teams. Unfortunately, not every class delivers the same result. Before you pay, check these seven things.

## 1. Who issues the certificate?

The most important question. To work in Indonesia under [Regulation 9/2016](/en/permenaker-9-2016-work-at-height/), you need a **competency certificate and K3 licence from the Indonesian Ministry of Manpower**. An internal certificate of attendance does not meet this requirement. See also [Kemnaker vs BNSP](/en/kemnaker-vs-bnsp-k3-certification/).

## 2. An official delivery route

Ask which PJK3 the provider works with and how the exam process runs. A serious provider will explain the process openly, including the documents you need to prepare.

## 3. The right programme for your level

Make sure the programme matches your needs: [TKPK](/en/choosing-rope-access-certification/) for working suspended on rope, TKBT for climbing structures. Avoid "express" packages that promise to skip levels without experience.

## 4. Instructor-to-trainee ratio

Rope work needs close supervision. Ask how many trainees each instructor watches during practice at height.

## 5. Training facilities

Is there a tower or training structure for ascending, descending, rope transfers, passing knots, and edges? Practice only on a low wall does not prepare you for a real building.

## 6. Equipment and rescue practice

Equipment should be maintained and logged — a small sign of the provider's safety culture. Make sure there is hands-on [colleague rescue](/en/tested-rescue-plan/), not just theory.

## 7. Transparent total cost

Ask what is included: materials, equipment during training, the exam, certificate and licence issuance, meals. A low price that excludes certificate issuance often ends up costing more.
