The full body harness is the most-used item of fall protection — and the most often worn wrongly. A loose, twisted, or upside-down harness can let a body slip out or cause injury when it arrests a fall. Here is how to wear one correctly.

## 7 steps to put on a full body harness

1. **Inspect first.** Check the label (manufacture date, serial number, standard), run your fingers along all the webbing, and check stitching and buckles. Cuts, burns, or an unreadable label mean do not use it. Full guidance in [inspecting rope access PPE](/en/rope-access-ppe-inspection/).
2. **Hold it by the back D-ring.** Lift the harness by the dorsal D-ring and shake it so all straps hang straight and untwisted.
3. **Put on the shoulder straps.** Slip your arms in like a vest. The back D-ring must sit **between the shoulder blades**, not at the waist or the neck.
4. **Fasten the chest strap.** Connect it mid-chest, about armpit height. Too high can choke you in a fall; too low lets the body slip out over the top.
5. **Fasten the leg straps.** Pull each leg strap from back to front between the legs and buckle it. Make sure they are not crossed or twisted.
6. **Tighten everything.** Leg straps: a flat hand fits, a fist does not. Tuck loose strap ends into their keepers so they cannot snag.
7. **Do a short hang test.** If possible, hang briefly close to the ground. A well-fitted harness does not shift and does not press sharply into the groin.

## The mistakes we see most often

- **Loose leg straps** for comfort while walking — the most dangerous, because the body can slide down when a fall is arrested.
- **Chest strap too high** at the neck.
- **Lanyard clipped to a gear loop** instead of the attachment point marked A.
- **Someone else's harness** worn without readjusting it to your size.
- **No rescue plan** — long suspension in a harness can cause *suspension trauma*. Read [an untested rescue plan is not a plan](/en/tested-rescue-plan/).

## Rope access harnesses are different

For rope access, the harness also has a ventral attachment for the descender and ascenders, plus a seat for long suspension. See the [rope access equipment list](/en/rope-access-equipment-list/).
