We hear this question in almost every class: what does a rope access technician actually earn? Pay varies, but public job ads give a reasonably clear picture. The figures below are a **sample of ads we found in 2026**, not an official survey.

## Ranges from public job ads

| Role (example ad) | Monthly range | Stated requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rope access / gondola (contract) | IDR 3–4.7 million | High school, 1–3 years' experience |
| Gondola crew | IDR 5–6 million | 2–3 years in gondola/rope access |
| Work at height technician | IDR 5–6 million | Work-at-height K3 certificate (TKPK 2/TKBT 2) |

For comparison, Indeed lists the average base pay for technical roles in Indonesia at about IDR 5.1 million per month.

## What pushes pay up

- **Certificate grade.** Better-paid ads almost always name a Kemnaker certificate and K3 licence. Moving from [TKPK Level 1](/en/tkpk-level-1-training/) to [Level 2](/en/tkpk-level-2-training/) opens more complex work.
- **Sector.** Industrial work — oil and gas, power plants, tanks, towers — usually pays more than building maintenance, often with field allowances.
- **Extra skills.** Rope access technicians who can also do [NDT inspection](/en/ndt-inspection-services/), welding, industrial coating, or sealant work are in much higher demand.
- **Supervisory roles.** [TKPK Level 3](/en/tkpk-level-3-training/) technicians lead teams and plan rescues — more responsibility and higher pay.
- **Track record.** A logbook of hours and valid certificates make negotiating easier.

## A realistic career path

Most technicians start in building work, build up hours and experience, then step up a certificate grade and move into industrial or supervisory work. We cover the stages in [the rope access career ladder](/en/rope-access-career-indonesia/).
