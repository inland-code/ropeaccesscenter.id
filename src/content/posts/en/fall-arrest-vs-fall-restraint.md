In work at height, three terms are often used interchangeably even though they mean different things: **fall restraint**, **work positioning**, and **fall arrest**. Mixing them up can mean choosing the wrong equipment.

## Fall restraint: never reaching the edge

The worker is tethered by a rope or lanyard that is **too short to reach the edge**. Because they cannot get to the drop, no fall can happen. Example: a worker on a flat roof connected to a lifeline in the middle of the roof.

**Strength:** the safest option in the fall protection order. **Limit:** only suitable when the job does not require going near the edge.

## Work positioning: supported at the work spot

The worker is **held in position** so both hands are free, such as a tower climber leaning on a positioning lanyard, or a [rope access](/en/what-is-rope-access/) technician suspended on a working rope. It must always be backed up by fall arrest.

## Fall arrest: stopping a fall that has happened

A system that **stops a fall** after it starts: a [full body harness](/en/how-to-wear-full-body-harness/), a lanyard with an energy absorber or a fall arrester, and a strong [anchor point](/en/rope-access-anchor-points/). Two things must be planned:

- **Fall clearance.** Lanyard length, absorber extension, body height, and a safety margin must fit in the space below the worker. If not, the worker still hits the ground.
- **Rescue.** A worker left hanging after a fall must be brought down quickly. See [an untested rescue plan is not a plan](/en/tested-rescue-plan/).

## The order of choice

Following the planning principle in [Regulation 9/2016](/en/permenaker-9-2016-work-at-height/):

1. Avoid work at height if the job can be done from below.
2. Use collective protection (guardrails, platforms, scaffolding with rails).
3. Use **fall restraint** where possible.
4. Use **work positioning** with fall arrest backup.
5. Use **fall arrest** and prepare a rescue.
