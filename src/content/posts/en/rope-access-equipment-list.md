Rope access relies on equipment designed to hold a person for long periods and to arrest a fall if something fails. Here is what a technician's standard kit contains, what each item does, and the standards usually marked on the products.

## 1. Full body harness

The centre of the whole system. A rope access harness has a **chest** attachment (for fall arrest) and a **ventral** attachment (for descender and ascenders), plus a seat that keeps the technician comfortable for hours. It usually meets EN 361 (fall arrest harness) and EN 813 (sit harness). How to put one on is covered in [how to wear a full body harness](/en/how-to-wear-full-body-harness/).

## 2. Descender

The device for going down the **working rope**. The technician controls speed with a handle; releasing it locks the device (anti-panic and auto-lock on work models). Rope adjustment devices like this are tested to EN 12841.

## 3. Ascenders (chest and hand)

Rope clamps that only slide upward. Used as a pair — one on the chest, one in the hand with a foot loop — so the technician can "walk" up the rope.

## 4. Backup device (fall arrester)

A device that travels with the technician on the **safety rope** and locks immediately in a fall. This is what makes the [two-rope system](/en/what-is-rope-access/) truly redundant.

## 5. Carabiners and connectors

The links between components. For rope access use **locking** carabiners (screw-lock or auto-lock) to EN 362. Steel for anchors that see abrasion, aluminium to save weight.

## 6. Static kernmantle rope

Rope with a core (kern) and sheath (mantle) that **stretches very little** — typically EN 1891 Type A, around 10.5–11 mm. Dynamic climbing rope is not used as a working line.

## 7. Lanyards, slings, and rope protection

Lanyards connect the harness to devices and anchors. Slings and anchor straps are used to build [anchor points](/en/rope-access-anchor-points/). Rope protectors stop the rope rubbing on sharp edges — the most common cause of rope failure.

## 8. Helmet and other PPE

A helmet with chin strap (EN 12492, or EN 397 with chin strap), gloves, safety boots, and eye protection as the job requires. Chemical or hot work needs additional PPE.

## Before you buy

Buy from authorised distributors, keep proof of purchase and serial numbers, and log every item in a [PPE inspection system](/en/rope-access-ppe-inspection/). In our [TKPK Level 1](/en/tkpk-level-1-training/) class, trainees practise with all of this equipment before deciding what to buy.
