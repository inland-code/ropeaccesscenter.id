Client audits rarely fail because of poor field practice. Far more often, they fail because good practice cannot be evidenced on paper.

## Nine documents to have ready

1.  An HSE policy signed by top management and still in date
2.  A risk assessment specific to each type of work at height
3.  A written [rescue plan](/en/standby-rescue-services/) together with records of it being tested
4.  Competency certificates for all personnel, with verified validity dates
5.  A [PPE](/en/rope-access-ppe-inspection/) master register with serial numbers and [inspection](/en/ndt-inspection-services/) history
6.  Six-monthly detailed inspection records signed by a competent person
7.  An anchor point register with safe working load calculations
8.  The work-at-height permit procedure and completed sample permits
9.  Incident and near-miss records with their follow-up actions

## The three most often missing

**Rescue plan test records.** The plan exists; evidence it was ever run does not. A thorough auditor will ask for the date, the elapsed time, and who took part.

**The anchor point register.** Many facilities use long-standing anchor points with no load calculation on file. The auditor’s question is always the same: who declared this point safe, and on what basis?

**PPE history.** The kit looks clean and well kept, but nobody can show when it was purchased, how many times it has been inspected, or who inspected it.

## How to close the gaps quickly

All three can be fixed in weeks rather than months. Schedule one rescue plan test and record the result. Commission an anchor survey by a competent party and publish the register. Start the PPE history from today, noting honestly that data before that date is unavailable — auditors value candour paired with corrective action far more highly than a record back-filled to look complete.
