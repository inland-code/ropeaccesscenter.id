Building managers who need windows cleaned or a facade repaired usually face two options: a **gondola** or **rope access**. Both are legitimate and safe when done properly. What separates them is the shape of the building, the type of work, and how often it is needed.

## Quick comparison

| Aspect | Gondola | Rope access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Needs roof rails/machine or a rented gondola | Only suitable anchor points |
| Reach | Flat, straight facades | Straight facades, curves, canopies, corners, atriums |
| Spot work/repairs | Less efficient (machine must be moved) | Very efficient |
| Routine cleaning of large areas | Efficient if a permanent gondola exists | Efficient; more teams can be added |
| Impact below | Exclusion zone below | Exclusion zone below, smaller footprint |
| Worker competency | Gondola operator | Certified TKPK technician |

## When to choose a gondola

A gondola makes most sense on buildings with a **permanent gondola** already installed and flat glass facades cleaned on a routine schedule. The platform has room for plenty of tools and materials.

## When to choose rope access

- Buildings **without a gondola**, or where the gondola is out of service.
- Facades with **recesses, canopies, fins, or louvres** that block a gondola.
- **Spot work**: replacing one glass panel, [leaking glass sealant](/en/maintenance-repair-at-height/), ACP repairs, or [facade inspection](/en/ndt-inspection-services/).
- Urgent schedules — a rope access team can start as soon as anchors and the permit to work are ready.

The cost logic mirrors [rope access versus scaffolding](/en/rope-access-vs-scaffolding-inspection-cost/): the less you have to install, the faster and cheaper the job.

## Equally important: safety

Whatever the method, the work falls under [Regulation 9/2016](/en/permenaker-9-2016-work-at-height/): planning, procedures, equipment, and competent workers. For rope access, make sure technicians hold a valid TKPK certificate and that there is a [rescue plan](/en/tested-rescue-plan/).
